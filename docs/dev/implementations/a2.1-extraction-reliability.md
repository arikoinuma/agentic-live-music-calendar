## Context

A2 rebuilt the extraction schema and measured it. The schema change fixed what it was written for (invented
start times, non-music leaks), but Ari judged the overall quality unacceptable on October 10, 2026, and a later
recount found it had **broken act splitting** (A2-F9: multi-act listings come back as one act named with the whole
title — Zhora ~17 of 23 split → 0). Ari set the bar (A2 Decision 9, refined October 10 into the tiered standard in D1): **at least 95% of shows on the labelled pages
extracted with the correct date, time and acts.** A2.1 exists to reach that bar, or to show precisely why it
can't be reached with the current approach.

### What A2.1 delivers

1. **A measurement of Ari's accuracy standard** (D1). Hand-written labels for a gated set of pages, a scorer for its
   tiers (date and acts exact, no invented shows, per-field missing vs. wrong, tolerated non-music leaks), and a
   gate computed per run. The stored A2 results are rescored
   against it for free.
2. **Fixtures that match production**: fresh captures made by the pipeline's own fetch, alongside the August ones.
3. **Structured-first extraction**: a generic schema.org JSON-LD reader, converted into the metro's wall clock
   (A2 Decision #3), with the model narrowed to the one judgement JSON-LD doesn't make — splitting an event title
   into acts and screening out non-music entries.
4. **Model-path fixes, measured one at a time**: the A2-F9 act-splitting fix, the sampling temperature (A2-S11), and the
   `free_admission: false` coercion (A2-F1).
5. **One extraction entry point** shared by `extract_node` and the eval harness, so the eval keeps measuring what
   the pipeline sends (the drift A2's `prompts.py` fixed for the instruction now extends to request options and
   routing).

*Each has a recommendation. D1, D2, D5, D11 and D13 shape the measurement and are needed before any gated run; the

**D1 — The accuracy standard and how it is scored. DECIDED (Ari, October 10, 2026), replacing the single 95% bar.**
Ari's standard is tiered; it is recorded centrally in `requirements-initial.md` ("Extraction accuracy standards"),
and this plan scores exactly that:

| Tier | Standard (per run, on the sample venues) | What the scorer counts |
|---|---|---|
| **Show identity: date and acts** | **100%.** Every real show on the page is found, with the right date and the right acts. | *missed* = a labelled show with no returned show of the same date and correct acts; *acts wrong* = matched on date, acts don't equal the label (or any of its alternatives). Both must be 0 |
| **No invented shows** | **Never.** | *fabricated* = a returned show (with acts) that matches no labelled show and no labelled non-show entry. Must be 0 |
| **Other fields** — start time, end time, admission advance / day, free admission, image link, status (D12) | **Missing** a value the page states: at most **1 in 10**. **Wrong or invented**: **never**. | Per field, over the labelled shows that matched: *stated*, *correct*, *missing* (page states it, returned null), *wrong* (returned a different value, or a value where the page states none). wrong = 0; missing ≤ 10% of stated |
| **Non-music entries** | An occasional leak is tolerated: at most **about one per month per venue**. | *leak* = a returned show matching a labelled non-show entry (karaoke, open mic, comedy, a recurring header…). ≤ 1 per venue page (a page is about a month) |

Scoring mechanics:
- **Matching is on date, not (date, time)** — time is now a field with its own tolerance, so a missed time must
  not turn a found show into a missed one. K2 matches each labelled show to a returned show on the same date,
  preferring one whose acts are correct, then one whose time agrees, then the first; each returned show is used
  once.
- Act names are compared as sets after normalising case, Unicode form, HTML entities, whitespace and typographic
  punctuation (’ ‘ → `'`, “ ” → `"`, – — → `-`, NBSP → space; review #5). Order is reported, not gated. Ambiguous
  titles carry `alternatives` (D5).
- A returned show with `acts: []` is dropped before counting and reported on its own (review #4).
- **Every run must meet every tier** — 5 runs per page, seeds 0–4, at any temperature (review #14); a run that raises
  fails. The "missing ≤ 10%" rate is pooled per run across the gated pages (a 12-show page can't express 10% on its
  own); everything else is counted per page. Whether a label's runs were identical is recorded.
- Reported per route (`jsonld` / `model`) as well as overall (review #15).
- The labels therefore record, per show, the fields the page states (`time`, `end_time`, prices, free admission,
  status) — `None` meaning "the page doesn't state it", so any returned value there is *wrong* — and, per page,
  the **non-show entries** with their dates (or weekday, for a recurring header), so an extra can be classified as
  a tolerated leak or an intolerable fabrication.

**D5 — Labelling conventions for acts.** The labels decide what "correct acts" means, so their judgement calls are
Ari's. *Recommendation* (each with an example from the pages):
- `X w. Y, Z` and `X w/ Y` → `X`, `Y`, `Z` ("Lent w. Gills, Boomdagger").
- `A / B` → `A`, `B` (Terminal Bar's "Griffin / Alec C").
- `&` inside a name stays: "Martin Devaney & Friends", "Wesley Church & the Fabulous Vanguards" are one act each.
- A series or event title is not an act; its performers are: "Alex & Toke's (Pullstring) Acoustic Tuesdays: Ft.
  Joan Of Profile, Meghan Kreidler (Kiss The Tiger)" → `Joan Of Profile`, `Meghan Kreidler` (the hosts' own
  performance is a judgement call — flag it).
- Parenthetical band affiliations are dropped from the name: "Meghan Kreidler (Kiss The Tiger)" → `Meghan Kreidler`.
- ~~Hosts are not acts~~ *Reversed by Ari, October 11:* **a named host of a music show is one of its acts**
  ("Terminal Fest 2 hosted by Andrew Himmer" → `Andrew Himmer`; "Alex & Toke's … Acoustic Tuesdays" → `Alex & Toke`
  among the acts). Being "hosted by" someone still doesn't make a karaoke night, open mic or comedy night a show.
  A presenter ("X Presents:") is a promoter, not an act.
- A tour or album-release name is not an act: "Owen Riegling | In The Feeling Tour | with | Payton Smith" →
  `Owen Riegling`, `Payton Smith`; "Absolutely Yours Album Release Show w/ Embahn + The Vanity Project" → the band
  releasing and the named acts (flag it — whether "Absolutely Yours" is the band is a judgement call).
- `+` separates acts like `,` ("SUPPORTIVE PARENTS + VIRGIN WHORES + DAISYCUTTER").
- A named DJ in a list is an act ("SPELLBOUND w/ DJ Gwiingwans, Coagula, and PRSPHNE" → `DJ Gwiingwans`, `Coagula`,
  `PRSPHNE`; "SPELLBOUND" is a night's name). A title that mixes a show with a non-music element ("… + Karaoke")
  keeps the show and drops the non-music part.
- *Added in round 2 (#12), each flagged for Ari:* `•` and "featuring" / "ft." separate acts like `,`; "live visuals
  by X" is not an act (a visual artist isn't a performing act — flag); a tag such as "★ Local Show ★" is not part of
  any name; **every** parenthetical is dropped, not only band affiliations ("Philip Bowen (solo)" → `Philip Bowen`,
  "Boss's Daughter (Reno)" → `Boss's Daughter`); a B2B DJ set "Flange August Back to Back" → `Flange August` as one
  act (flag — or two DJs); a night's name before "W/" is not an act ("POPUP! W/ SAM WOODMAN" → `SAM WOODMAN`); a tour
  name is removed *before* splitting, so markers inside it don't split ("I Don't Kiss & Tell, I Kiss & Tour").
- **"w/" introduces opening acts** (Ari, October 10, revising an earlier answer): it more likely separates acts than
  belongs to a band name, so the conventions keep splitting at it. A billing name can still bind several people into
  one act: "Slash and the Conspirators Ft. Myles Kennedy" is one act (an artist, his backing band and a featured
  singer), which no splitting rule can see — a case for the artist-display enhancement, and for `alternatives` when a
  label meets one.
- **Acts come from the listing title only; description text never adds or splits acts** (review #2). Both routes
  see the title (the JSON-LD `name`, the page's heading line); a label that used the description would disagree
  with them by construction. Where the title spells a name differently from the description ("Dread Spell" /
  "Dread Spells"), the title wins.
- **Genuinely ambiguous titles get alternative readings, not a forced choice.** "Slashfire w. Dread Spell & Couch
  Potato Massacre" is two bands joined by `&` (per the event's description), which no title-only rule can tell
  apart from "Martin Devaney & Friends"; "Lord Jeremy w/ Enemies w. The Limns" may contain a band named "Lord Jeremy
  w/ Enemies". Such rows list each acceptable act set (`alternatives`), and the scorer accepts any of them. Ari
  decides which rows are ambiguous and what their readings are.
- **Not shows, so not labelled:** recurring undated header entries ("OPEN MIC Every Monday", "DJ Night Every
  Wednesday"); "Open Jam" (an open jam names no performing artist — treated like open mic); a "hosted by" event
  with no named acts. A returned show for any of these is an extra — a tolerated leak if it is recognisably that
  entry, a fabrication otherwise. (*Cancelled and postponed listings ARE labelled, with their status — D12 as decided;
  this bullet said otherwise before code review #9.*)
I draft the labels by reading each page's text (never from JSON-LD or a model's output), quoting the source line
beside each show, and mark every row where a convention was applied; Ari confirms those rows before the first
gated run.

**D12 — Cancelled and postponed listings. DECIDED (Ari, October 10, 2026), changing the recommendation.** They are
not shows to *display*, but they **must be extracted**: unless a venue is being scanned for the first time, the
show was probably extracted before it was cancelled, and reconcile has to match it to the stored record and update
it. So the extraction carries a **`status`** (`scheduled` / `cancelled` / `postponed`, default `scheduled`) with
enough identity (date, time, acts) to match an existing show. On the JSON-LD route `eventStatus` maps to it
deterministically ([written]); on the model route a field description plus one sentence in the cross-cutting
rules (`prompts._cancelled_rule()`: "a listing marked cancelled or postponed is still extracted, with its status")
— its own rung, `a21-cancelled`. Labels include cancelled/postponed shows with their status; a wrong status is a
*wrong value* (D1: never). **What reconcile then does** with a cancelled match (soft delete, or a status shown in
the UI) is A5's, recorded there; the data model is not changed in A2.1.

**D13 — Supplying the year. DECIDED (Ari, October 10, 2026): in code, not in the prompt.** A date's year is set by a
rule, not by the model: given the fetch date *F*, a listed month/day takes *F*'s year, unless that date would fall
more than a month before *F*, in which case it takes the next year (scanning January listings in November or
December). Implemented as `normalize.infer_year(month_day, fetch_date)`-style post-processing on the model route
(and the image route — it fixes the posters' 2023/2024 too), switched by `ExtractionConfig.fetch_date`; rung
`a21-year`. The pipeline passes the run's date (`fetch_node` records it); the harness passes each fixture's
capture date (`PageLabel.captured_on`; the August fixtures were saved August 17, 2026). It overrides the model's
year even where the page states one — on a page of upcoming listings the rule gives the same answer, and it removes
a class of guess. *Known limit:* an archive page listing shows more than a month in the past would be pushed a year
ahead; calendar pages list upcoming shows, and past shows are outside what reconcile touches. The JSON-LD route
needs none of this (its dates carry the year). A3 keeps a plausibility check as a backstop.

## Design

### 1. One extraction entry point (`agent_worker/extraction.py`, new)

Today `extract_node` and `eval/run_eval.extract` each build their own request (model, `max_tokens`,
`max_retries`, `num_ctx`); only the instruction text is shared. A2.1 adds a temperature, a routing decision and a
second model call, so the whole request moves into one module both call:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class PageExtractionResult:
    extraction: VenuePageExtraction
    source: Literal["jsonld", "model"]        # which route ran — recorded for the eval now, the audit trail in A4
    notes: dict[str, Any]                      # e.g. JSON-LD event count, titles the splitter dropped

def extract_page(html: str, *, venue_name: str, metro_timezone: str,
                 client: Instructor, options: RequestOptions, config: ExtractionConfig,
                 seed: int | None = None) -> PageExtractionResult
def extract_image(image_b64: str, mime: str, *, client, options, seed=None) -> VenuePageExtraction   # posters, unchanged behaviour

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class RequestOptions:                          # one place for what every model call sends
    model: str
    temperature: float | None = None           # None = the model's own default (today's behaviour)
    max_tokens: int = 16000
    max_retries: int = 3
    num_ctx: int = 32768                       # still sent; see notes.md on /v1 ignoring it

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class ExtractionConfig:                        # the ladder's switches (plan review #1, #3, #16)
    route: Literal["auto", "model"] = "model"  # "auto" = K3's routing; "model" forces the full-page model route
    keep_line_breaks: bool = False             # cleaning: get_text(separator="\n") instead of " "
    include_new_rules: bool = True             # False = A2's `schema-only` instruction (--old-rules survives)
    status_field: bool = False                 # D12: the schema with `status` + the cancelled sentence; its own rung
    year_rule: bool = False                    # D13: set each date's year from the fetch date, in code
    act_conventions: bool = False              # round 2 #2: D5's conventions sent on the model route too
```

**Every switch defaults to today's behaviour**, so `a21-rebaseline` really is "current code" and each ladder label
flips exactly one thing (review #3). When a label is adopted (step 8), the *pipeline's* value flips — `extract_node`
builds its `ExtractionConfig` and `RequestOptions` from one module-level pair in `extraction.py`, so the adopted
configuration is one diff, the same way D8 sets the temperature. **The harness starts from that same module-level
pair** and each flag overrides one field (`--route`, `--line-breaks`, `--old-rules`, `--temperature`,
`--status`, `--act-conventions`, `--year-rule`), so after a rung is adopted, later labels measure the
adopted pipeline without repeating its flag (round 2 #5). Every results file records the full `ExtractionConfig` and
`RequestOptions`, and `--compare` prints them in its provenance block.

- `extract_page` does the cleaning itself (moved from `fetch_node`), because the JSON-LD route needs the **raw
  HTML** — `html_clean` strips `<script>` blocks, and JSON-LD lives in them. There is one cleaning function,
  `clean_text(html, keep_line_breaks)`, used by the pipeline and the harness (`run_eval.load_text_fixture` becomes a
  call to it), and the text actually sent is stored in each eval run record, so step 9 can tell "the boundary was in
  the text and the model ignored it" from "the boundary was never sent" (review #1).
- `VenueGraphState` gains `page_html: str | None` (raw), `venue_name: str` and `metro_timezone: str`. The last two
  are facts from the venue's database row; the worker doesn't read the database yet (A4/A5), so whoever builds the
  state passes them (`local_test.py`: the seed's "The White Squirrel Bar", `"America/Chicago"`, with a comment).
  **No default zone is hidden in the extraction code**: `metro.timezone` is the source of truth (A2 Decision #3).
  `page_content` stays (cleaned text, or base64 for an image fixture) so `local_test` output and the image path
  are unchanged.
- The image path keeps today's behaviour, including the `image/png` label on every image (pre-existing noise,
  A2 Step 1); `extract_image` takes a `mime` argument so a later fix is one line, but A2.1 passes `image/png` to
  stay comparable.
- The eval's `--legacy` route keeps its own frozen `extract` (A2's baseline must stay re-runnable).

### 2. The JSON-LD route (`agent_worker/sources/jsonld.py`, new)

- `find_events(html) -> list[dict]`: every `<script type="application/ld+json">` block, parsed with `json.loads`;
  objects collected from a single object, a list, or an `@graph`; kept when `@type` (a string or a list) is `Event`
  or a schema.org subtype ending in `Event` (`MusicEvent`); an unparseable block is skipped, never raised (one bad
  block on a page must not cost the others). Document order kept.
- `local_wall_clock(value, metro_timezone) -> tuple[date, time | None]` — **TODO(Ari) K1**. A date-only value
  (`"2026-10-09"`) gives `(date, None)`; a value with an offset or `Z` is **converted** into the metro's zone; a
  naive value is already local. White Squirrel sends `-05:00`, DICE-backed pages send `Z`.
- `to_structured_show(event, metro_timezone) -> StructuredShow`: `startDate` → date and time; `endDate` → end time
  only when it is at most 6 hours after the start (D10), its date otherwise ignored ("9:00 pm – 12:00 am" ends the
  next day; A3's end-before-start bullet covers it); `name` →
  title, HTML-unescaped (White Squirrel's JSON-LD carries `&#038;` and `&#8216;`); `image` (a string, a list, or an
  `ImageObject` with `url`) → `image_url`; `offers` (one or a list) → a price per D7, `free_admission` only if an
  offer price is explicitly `0`; otherwise null.
- `split_titles(titles, *, client, options) -> list[list[str]]` (`agent_worker/act_split.py`, new): **one** model
  call per page with a small response model — `[{id, title, acts: [str]}]`, the titles sent numbered — whose
  instruction is the narrow task
  (**TODO(Ari) K4**): split each title into the performing acts per D5's conventions, and return **no acts** for a
  title that isn't a music show with a performing artist (reusing `prompts._non_music_rule()`; JSON-LD lists every
  event on the venue's calendar, karaoke included, so the exclusion rule must still run on this route). Results
  are aligned to the input **by `id`, not by position**, so a dropped or reordered item can't shift every act list
  onto the wrong show; the echoed title is a cross-check only (compared after `normalise_act`-style folding, since a
  model may re-quote or re-escape it — review #10), and a mismatch is recorded in `notes`. An id missing from the
  answer gets no acts and is recorded in `notes`.
- Each structured show becomes a `ShowExtraction`: `venue=venue_name`; date/time/end/image/price from JSON-LD; `acts`
  from the splitter (no per-act start times — JSON-LD has none; `sequence_number` null, so A5 uses list order);
  `is_ambiguous=False`; `confidence=1.0` — a **placeholder**: it claims a certainty about acts the splitter doesn't
  have, and `validate_node` routes on it; A3's decision on combining checks with confidence replaces it (recorded
  there, review #15). A show whose splitter result is empty is **kept with `acts: []`** —
  A3's completeness check drops it, exactly as on the model route (A2 Design, "Consequences"), so both routes hand
  A3 the same shapes.
- `eventStatus` `EventCancelled` / `EventPostponed` (a full schema.org URL or the bare name) maps to `status`
  `cancelled` / `postponed`; anything else, or absent, is `scheduled` (D12). The show is kept — reconcile needs it.
- **Field names are schema.org's, not the site's** (Ari asked, October 10). JSON-LD's `Event` vocabulary
  (`name`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `eventStatus`, `offers`, `image`) is a published standard, so the reader maps it
  in code with no model involved: that determinism is the point of the route. What varies between sites is *shape*
  (an object or a list, `image` as a string or an `ImageObject`, `Z` vs. an offset), which the reader handles and
  `test_jsonld.py` pins. A page whose events lack `name` or `startDate` isn't usable and falls back to the model
  route. Platform-specific data that isn't schema.org (Mortimer's Wix data) needs its own reader — deferred, not
  guessed at by a model.
- `page_readable=True` whenever usable events were found.

Each experiment is one eval label: the gated set plus the informational set, N runs per D1, scored and gated,
with provenance (Ollama version, model digest, request options, extraction config). **One change per label**, kept
if it improves the **worst-run pooled score** (D1) without losing more than one correct show on any single page;
otherwise reverted. Results
go in this file's "Experiment results" table as they come in.

| Label | Change | Why in this order |

## Risks

- **The gated set is modest** (~75 shows over six pages, four venues), and D1 asks for **exact** dates and acts on
  every run.
  It is what production can reach today; more venues widen it later (A8). D1's pooling makes the bar meaningful at
  this size; it doesn't make it statistically strong, and the result will say so.
- **Runtime.** Six gated plus three informational pages (Zhora, two posters; the `.ics` is skipped) × 5 runs × ~2
  minutes is about 1.5 hours per label, and the ladder has up to eight labels — roughly 12 hours of background runs,
  one label at a time. The JSON-LD route's pages are much faster (one short splitter call). If runtime matters more
  than attribution, the three prompt-only rungs (`a21-year`, `a21-cancelled`, `a21-act-conventions`) are the ones to
  batch — Ari's call, not a default.
- **Date-after-title pairing.** On Mortimer's the date follows its title; in round 2's probe the model paired five
  titles with the *previous* entry's date. `a21-newlines` may fix it (each entry's lines become adjacent); if it
  doesn't, it is a model-route cleaning problem for D9 (A2-S2), not something a rung here addresses.
- **Labels carry judgement** (D5), and a scorer is only as right as its labels. Mitigated by `source_line` on every
  row, `alternatives` for genuinely ambiguous titles, and Ari confirming the flagged rows. Some titles can't be split
  correctly from the title alone (Slashfire); alternatives keep that from reading as a model failure, but it is a
  real limit of title-only extraction.
- **JSON-LD can under-report or be stale** relative to the visible page. Gated recall catches it on the gated pages;
  production needs a guard (recorded for A3).
- **The splitter is a new model call** with its own failure modes (dropped titles, invented acts). Alignment by
  title and the `notes` record make drops visible; the gate measures the rest.
- **The environment can drift again** (Ollama updates itself; notes.md). Every label records version and digest;
  `--compare` shows them; a mismatch means re-run, not compare.
- **Routing inside `extract_node` rather than a graph node.** A separate "route" node would make the decision visible
  in the graph, but A3 is about to rework the retry loop around `extract`, and the audit trail (A4) can record
  `source` either way. Kept inside for now; revisit in A3/A4.
- **Temperature 0 may reduce quality** on a small model (repetition, worse recall). That is what the label measures;
  D8 adopts by score, not by assumption.
- **October captures go stale** — the pages change daily. That is fine: a capture is a frozen fixture, labelled once;
  re-capturing means re-labelling.
